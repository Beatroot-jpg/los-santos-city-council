Good Roleplay Examples

The following scenarios demonstrate how the Los Santos City Council creates engaging, thought-provoking roleplay opportunities that challenge players to think beyond violence and engage with the city's systems in meaningful ways.

Scenario 1: Parking Enforcement & Consequences

The Situation

Tim decides he wants to visit a clothing store. He hops in his supercar and flies down to Strawberry Avenue Clothing Store. Instead of parking in the designated car park, he parks in front of the hydrant, half on the curb and half on the road. He proceeds into the store.

Two councilmen on patrol spot the car and decide it's not complying with a local law they just passed via a judge. They proceed to put a wheel clamp on Tim's car and await his arrival to conduct some RP with him.

The Interaction

Tim approaches the councilmen and asks what the hell is going on. The councilmen proceed to educate Tim on the local law. Tim tells them to get the clamp off or he's going to shoot them in the face. They exclaim that he needs to calm down or they'll call the police. The only way to have the clamp removed is to pay the small citation fee (something minute like $2,500—small but enough to make them think).

The Decision

Tim has to stop and think about it. If he shoots them now, he ain't getting the wheel clamp off. Or he can try to talk his way out of it and use his head as opposed to violence for a change. He decides to pay, so the councilmen take the clamp off and walk away. Tim proceeds to shoot them both in the back of the head.

✅ Conclusion

Councilmen: Happy they did their job and got a laugh out of it.
Tim: Happy he got to shoot the smug bastards in the back of the head, and he used a different part of his brain.

Everyone wins—the RP happened, consequences were considered, and the interaction was memorable.

Scenario 2: Legislative Process & Community Involvement

The Proposal

L.S.C.C decides they want to impose a new set of laws. They formulate their ideas and compile a proposal to take to the court for approval. They require the input of the police as it regards impounding vehicles and need insight on current procedures so they don't impose or make the standard harder for them.

Cross-Department Collaboration

They head down and request a meeting with Station Sergeant Tim to discuss the law. The SGT gives his opinion and seeks small amendments before it is proposed. The councilmen make the amendments and call a lawyer. They discuss the proposal with the lawyer and request a court date with Judge John to have the law approved or denied.

Public Court Process

Court is public, and people are advised via the court case channel in the Discord. People can attend and view the law being implemented. Judge makes an unbiased decision and passes the law as legal. It's then posted in the IC-Announcements channel in Discord.

Community Response

Tim sees this and is not happy with the law. He calls the city council and requests a town meeting so that it can be changed. Councilmen agree to a date and time and announce a city hall meeting where people can express their concerns. They then take the information to the lawyer and request another court case, and so on and so forth.

✅ Conclusion

Everyone gets to be involved in the scenario and can make an impact via following the correct procedures and actually thinking about how they can make a proper change. Makes for a smarter community.

  • Police: Consulted and involved in the process
  • Lawyers: Engaged in legal proceedings
  • Judges: Make meaningful decisions
  • Citizens: Have a voice and can effect change
  • Council: Facilitates democratic process

Initial RP Focus

Our initial roleplay focus will be centered on parking enforcement—a foundational aspect of city governance that creates immediate, tangible interactions with citizens while establishing the council's presence and authority.

Parking Law Framework

Proposed Parking Violations

We would script comprehensive laws regarding parking infractions, including but not limited to:

  • Facing Toward Incoming Traffic: Vehicles parked against the flow of traffic
  • Parking on the Curb: Vehicles partially or fully mounted on sidewalks
  • Blocking a Driveway: Obstructing private or commercial property access
  • Blocking a Fire Hydrant: Parking within prohibited distance of emergency equipment
  • No Parking Zones: Violations of designated restricted areas
  • Handicap Zone Violations: Unauthorized parking in accessible spaces

The Proper RP Route

We wouldn't just start enforcing rules arbitrarily. We'd go down the proper RP route to have our idea come to fruition before conducting patrols and issuing citations:

1️⃣ Law Drafting

Formulate comprehensive parking ordinances with clear definitions and penalties.

2️⃣ Stakeholder Consultation

Meet with police to ensure alignment with existing procedures and enforcement capabilities.

3️⃣ Legal Review

Present proposals to lawyers and request a court date for judicial approval.

4️⃣ Public Announcement

Once approved, announce the new laws through IC channels, giving citizens notice.

5️⃣ Grace Period

Allow time for citizens to learn the new regulations before full enforcement begins.

6️⃣ Active Enforcement

Begin patrols, issue citations, and engage in parking enforcement roleplay.

Custom Wheel Clamp Script

🔧 Fully Functional & Persistent

Not to mention, we've actually created a wheel clamp script that works and is persistent through restarts. This means once a vehicle is clamped, it stays clamped until properly removed—no exploits, no workarounds.

How It Works

  • Persistent Clamping: Wheel clamps remain on vehicles even after server restarts, ensuring consequences can't be avoided.
  • Multi-Department Access: People will need to contact Police, EMS, or the Council to have clamps removed.
  • Payment Required: Clamps can only be removed after paying the associated citation fee.
  • Visual Indicator: Clear visual representation of the clamp on the vehicle.

Cross-Department Integration

We want EMS and Police to have this item in their kit as well, so that it isn't just limited to us. This creates additional roleplay opportunities and practical applications:

🚔 Police Department Usage ▼

Pursuit Consequences: Maybe even having police get an SOP to clamp vehicles that are water-dumped during pursuits. This adds a realistic consequence to failed getaways.

Evidence Preservation: Clamp vehicles at crime scenes to prevent tampering or removal.

Hefty Fines: They can pay a hefty fine to have it removed, creating financial consequences for criminal activity.

🚑 EMS Department Usage ▼

Emergency Access: Clamp vehicles blocking ambulance bays or emergency access routes.

Scene Safety: Secure vehicles at accident scenes to prevent movement during medical operations.

Service Calls: Respond to clamp removal requests, creating additional RP interactions.

🏛️ City Council Usage ▼

Parking Enforcement: Primary use for parking violations and code enforcement.

Building Inspections: Clamp vehicles at properties under investigation or inspection.

Citation Collection: Ensure fines are paid before removing clamps.
